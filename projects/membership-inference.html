Membership Inference on GPT-2

Can you tell whether a sentence was in a language model's fine-tuning data just by querying the model? Our attack compares a fine-tuned GPT-2 against ten shadow models and reaches an AUC of 0.96 on the held-out test set, with no separate classifier.

01Question

A membership inference attack asks whether a specific record was used to train a model. Models tend to memorize their training data, so a successful attack can reveal that someone's health, financial or personal text was in the training set, which is exactly the kind of leak regulations like GDPR are meant to prevent.

Strong attacks like Shokri's shadow models and Carlini's LiRA need extra classifiers or reference models on top of shadow training. We asked whether simple statistics over a set of shadow models could produce a strong membership signal on their own.

02Method

  1. 01Shadow data

    10,000 cleaned WikiText-103 sentences per shadow model, each with a different seed

  2. 02Shadow models

    10 GPT-2 LoRA models (r = 32, α = 64) trained with the target's fine-tuning setup

  3. 03Features

    Average per-token cross-entropy loss and perplexity, from the target and every shadow

  4. 04Score

    How far the target's loss falls below the shadow mean, in standard deviations, averaged over both features

  5. 05Evaluate

    Continuous scores submitted to a Codabench leaderboard for AUC and TPR at 1% FPR

03Findings

  • Fine-tuned models leak membership

    A LoRA fine-tuned GPT-2 gives members a clearly lower loss than the shadow models predict, so membership can be read straight off the model.

  • More shadows, sharper signal

    Going from 6 to 10 shadow models raised the true positive rate at 1% false positives from 52.7% to 62.3% on the training set.

  • No classifier needed

    The averaged z-score is the membership score, so there is no extra attack model to train or tune.

04Takeaways

  • Teams fine-tuning on sensitive data

    A cheap attack like this works well against a LoRA fine-tune, so run one as a privacy audit before releasing the model.

  • Anyone evaluating an attack

    Report the true positive rate at a low false positive rate, not just AUC. The validation AUC barely moved, but the low-FPR rate shows how many members can be identified with confidence.

05Limitations & ethics

Per-token loss is affected by how hard a sentence is: rare words have high loss whether or not they were trained on, which blurs the signal. Every extra feature or shadow model adds training cost, and we didn't calibrate a decision threshold.

The attack ran only on models and data provided for the course competition, plus public WikiText-103 text. No personal data was involved.
