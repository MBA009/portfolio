Project 009 / 010

Ads on Kids' Google Accounts

Do Google's supervised child accounts really keep targeted and age-inappropriate ads away from kids? We ran one adult and two child profiles side by side. The child profiles saw only contextual ads, and almost none when they searched for vaping or marijuana.

Year

01Question

Parents set up supervised Google accounts trusting that they shield children from ad targeting. COPPA restricts collecting personal data from children under 13 without parental consent, which rules out profile-based ads but still allows contextual ones. Earlier studies found trackers on about 90% of child-directed websites and targeted ads on 27%.

We asked two questions. Do child profiles get any targeted ads beyond what the search itself suggests? And how do the ads differ when a child and an adult run the same "adult" searches?

02Method

  1. 01Set up

    Wiped Chrome data and created three accounts: adult A1, and children K1 and K2 (ages 11–12) through a verified parent account

  2. 02Prime

    Two weeks of age-appropriate Google searches and YouTube viewing for each profile

  3. 03Probe

    A1 and K1 ran 15 identical adult queries (vaping, marijuana); K1 and K2 shared 15 kid queries

  4. 04Log

    100 entries per profile: advertiser, placement, stated reason and likely targeting factor

03Findings

3Google profiles
1/15Adult searches that showed a child an ad
100Logged results per profile
  • Children get contextual ads, not behavioral ones

    Ads on both child profiles matched the search or video being viewed, such as toys and games, never a profile of the user.

  • Adult searches go almost ad-free

    Across 15 adult queries, the child account saw one ad, and it had nothing to do with the query. The adult saw ads on about half.

  • Child profiles stay blank

    Google's dashboard inferred the adult was single, high-income and not a parent. Both child dashboards stayed empty for the whole study.

  • Visible safeguards

    Child searches carry an extra flag in the URL, incognito mode is disabled, and parents get an email 30 days before the child turns 13.

  • Search and YouTube filter separately

    Some videos that Google Search showed the child were blocked when opened on YouTube, so the two systems aren't fully in sync.

04Recommendations

  • Parents

    Supervised accounts do block targeting and most adult ads, but children still see ads and filtering isn't perfect. Keep supervising.

  • Google

    Keep Search and YouTube filtering consistent, so results a child can't open aren't shown in the first place.

  • Researchers

    Test adult categories outside Google's restricted-ads policy, such as alcohol or gambling, and add an adult profile that browses like a child to separate account age from behavior.

05Limitations & ethics

Ads were logged by hand, which limited scale. Google already restricts tobacco and THC ads for everyone, so some of the child results may come from that general policy rather than from the child protections. The study covered only Google, over two weeks of priming and one week of collection.

The profiles were fictional and used no real person's data. The child accounts were created through Google's required parental process. We only visited public sites. Account names and identifiers are left off this page.

06Report

The full report, Investigating Ad Targeting Differences Between Child and Adult Google Profiles, covers the background on targeting mechanisms, the full logging scheme and the stakeholder analysis.
